# Prologue — The Bar

The hotel bar has decided it is elegant and is sticking to it. Low light, leather, a piano player working over a song Robert half remembers. He is three months retired, and he has learned that the hardest part of a Tuesday is the hour between seven and eight.

He orders a whiskey he does not particularly want. A man with a whiskey looks like a man with a routine.

The bar is half full. A conference crowd near the windows, two women splitting a dessert, a man his own age at the far end, nursing something amber and looking at nothing. Robert clocks him the way he clocks most things: quickly, without appearing to. Good shoes. No wedding ring. A face that has been handsome for a long time and is now something more interesting.

It takes forty minutes to end up next to him. Forty minutes of Robert pretending to read a menu, then asking about the steak, then agreeing that the steak is terrible, then disagreeing about whether the piano player is any good. The man's name is Gene. He is in town for a sales conference. He lives two states away. He has a dry laugh and a habit of holding his glass by the rim.

Robert says he is retired. Gene asks from what. Robert says, "Everything," and Gene laughs, and Robert feels the small warm click of being liked by a stranger.

They talk about nothing. A delayed flight. The hotel's ice, which is somehow always melting. A cab driver who took Gene the long way. Robert tells a story about a client who once tried to pay him in wine. Gene tells a story about a deal that fell through because someone's dog ate the contract. Robert is funny. Gene is easy. The piano player finishes and starts again.

At some point Gene says, "I'm going to use the restroom." He says it without emphasis. Robert waits a beat, then follows.

The bathroom is black tile and one bulb, the fan rattling like it is trying to escape. Gene locks the door. The bolt goes home, and something in Robert's chest unclenches.

Gene goes to his knees without ceremony. Robert's hand finds the back of his head. It is fast and wet and unromantic. Robert watches himself in the mirror, his own face doing something he does not quite recognize, and thinks, *There. That's something.*

Afterward Gene stands, wipes his mouth with the back of his wrist, and smiles like they have just finished a meeting.

"You're on the October crossing?" Gene says.

"What crossing?"

"Thirty days. No ports worth seeing. Everyone aboard is there for one reason."

Robert laughs. "What reason?"

Gene tucks his shirt back in. "You want the October sailing. Different crowd."

"What does that mean?"

"It means what it means." Gene washes his hands, carefully, the way a man washes his hands when he has all the time in the world. "Book it tonight. It fills up."

"Are you going?"

"I go every year." Gene meets his eyes in the mirror. "You won't remember me."

"I remember you."

Gene smiles. "You won't."

He leaves. The door swings shut. Robert stands alone in the black tile room, the fan rattling, his own reflection looking back at him with an expression he cannot name.

In the cab, he books the October crossing. The screen lights his face. He fills in the passenger details, pays the deposit, and puts the phone face down on his knee. The city slides past the window, all those lit rooms full of people he will never meet.

He feels like a man with a plan. It is a feeling he has had before, and it has never once been true.
